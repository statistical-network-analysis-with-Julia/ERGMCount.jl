## Project Overview

ERGMCount.jl is a Julia package for fitting Exponential Random Graph Models (ERGMs) to count-valued (integer-weighted) networks. It is a port of the R `ergm.count` package from the StatNet collection. Two estimators: the count MPLE (`method=:mple`; the exact MLE of a dyad-independent model, and **this package's** estimator — `ergm.count` has no MPLE) and Monte-Carlo maximum likelihood (`method=:mcmle`, `count_mcmle` — `ergm.count`'s estimator, pinned against it by `test/fixtures/count_mcmle.toml`). The default `method=:auto` follows R through `ERGM.resolve_method`: the MPLE for a dyad-independent formula, the MCMLE otherwise.

## Architecture

The entire package lives in a single file: `src/ERGMCount.jl`. It is organized into five sections:

1. **Reference Measures** -- Abstract type `AbstractReferenceMeasure` with concrete subtypes (`PoissonReference`, `GeometricReference`, `BinomialReference`, `DiscUnifReference`, `DiscUnif2Reference`). Each implements `log_reference(ref, y)`, `sample_reference(ref)`, and `_support(ref, max_val)`; `is_truncating(ref)` says whether `0:max_val` truncates it (Poisson/geometric) or is the model's own bound. Following `ergm.count`/Krivitsky (2012), `GeometricReference` is the counting measure h(y)=1 (no parameter) and `BinomialReference(trials)` is h(y)=C(trials,y) (no probability parameter) — the shape/probability is absorbed into the estimated `SumTerm` coefficient.

2. **Count-Specific Terms** -- Subtypes of `AbstractERGMTerm` (`import ERGM: name, compute, is_dyad_dependent, requires_directed`). Every term implements `name(t)`, `compute(t, net)`, and `change_stat_count(t, net, weights, i, j, old, new)` — the change in the statistic when dyad (i,j) moves from count `old` to `new`, holding other dyads fixed (implementations must not read the dyad's own current value). Both hot paths consume the change statistics as a **support profile**, `change_stats_support!(dest, term, net, weights, i, j, old, support)` (declared `public`): the fallback calls `change_stat_count` per value, and the strength terms (`_strength_profile!`: the strength excluding the dyad once, then `(s+y)² − (s+old)²`) and the triadic terms (`_tri_register!`/`_tri_integrate!`: the third-vertex minima `c_k` collected once over the neighbour intersections, then `Σ_k [min(y, c_k) − min(old, c_k)]` assembled for every `y` from a difference array in one pass) specialise it to O(degree + |support|) instead of O(degree × |support|). Every specialisation is held equal to the per-value definition by the testset "Support profiles equal the per-value change statistics"; `change_stat_count` stays the definition and stays brute-force-tested. Terms, labelled with the string R prints for the same term: dyad-independent `SumTerm(; pow=1)` (`sum`; `sum<p>` for R's `sum(pow=p)`, `pow > 0`, a non-integer power refused on negative counts where R gives NaN), `NonzeroTerm` (`nonzero`), `GreaterthannTerm(k)` (`greaterthan.k`), `CountAtleastnTerm(k)` (`atleast.k`), `AtmostTerm(k)` (`atmost.k`), `CMPTerm()` (`CMP`, Σ log y! — a **term** in ergm.count, never a reference; refused on negative counts where R gives Inf; its support profile is a one-pass cumulative log-factorial), `SmallerthanTerm(k)` (`smallerthan.k`, zero dyads counted), `EqualToTerm(v; tolerance)` (`equalto.v.pm.t`), `InIntervalTerm(a, b; open)` (`ininterval(a,b)` with R's bracket convention, `±Inf` bounds allowed); dyad-dependent `CountMutualTerm(form=:min; threshold)` (R's valued `mutual`: `mutual.min`/`mutual.nabsdiff`/`mutual.geom.mean`/`mutual.product`/`mutual.<t>`, one `_mutual_pair(t, a, b)` behind `compute` and `change_stat_count`), **Negative counts** (`_admits_negative`, `_refuse_negative_network`, `_validate_negative_terms`): on a network with a negative dyad weight (only `DiscUnif2Reference(a < 0, b)` admits one) `ergm` refuses `transitiveweights`/`cyclicalweights` ("Term may not be used with networks with negative dyad weights") and its `mutual(form="geometric")` is NaN, so `TransitiveWeightsTerm`, `CyclicalWeightsTerm` and `CountMutualTerm(:geometric)` are refused with R's sentence at `compute` (the summary statistic, as in R), at model construction and at `simulate_count_ergm` over a support reaching below 0 — never a number R does not produce (the `best = 0` floor of the two-path search would have defined one). `TransitiveWeightsTerm`/`CyclicalWeightsTerm` (R's `transitiveweights`/`cyclicalweights` with the default `(min, max, min)` triple: `Σ min(y_ij, max_k min(y_ik, y_kj))` over ordered pairs directed, unordered pairs undirected, where cyclical = transitive; `_best_twopath`/`_best_cycle_twopath(net, weights, i, j, skip)` find the strongest two-path leaving a named third vertex out, so the affected pairs `(i,l)`, `l ∈ out(j)`, and `(k,j)`, `k ∈ in(i)` — for the cycle `(a,i)`, `a ∈ out(j)`, and `(j,b)`, `b ∈ in(i)` — re-assemble their strength with the changing dyad at any value), `TransitiveTiesTerm`/`CyclicalTiesTerm` (**no R counterpart**: triple-wise minimum sums kept from 0.1, names `transitiveties.count`/`cyclicalties.count`, not validated against R), `NodeOSumTerm`, `NodeISumTerm`, `NodeSumTerm` (squared strengths; no R counterpart either — `ergm`'s `nodecovar` family is a covariance). **Covariate terms** (`_CountCovariateTerm`, dyad-independent: Σ x_ij·f(y_ij), f = y for `form=:sum`, 1[y ≠ 0] for `:nonzero`; one shared `compute`/`change_stat_count` over `_dyad_covariate(t, net, i, j)`): `CountNodeMatchTerm(attr; level, form)` (`nodematch.sum.g`, `….g.a` — one level of R's `diff=TRUE`), `CountNodeFactorTerm(attr, level; form)` (`nodefactor.sum.g.b`; R's term is one per level but the first), `CountAbsDiffTerm`, `CountNodeCovTerm`, `CountNodeOCovTerm`/`CountNodeICovTerm` (directed only), `CountEdgeCovTerm(W; name)` (`W[min,max]` undirected). `_validate_term_data` (called by `_validate_count_terms`) refuses a missing or non-numeric attribute and a wrong-sized `W`. They are not in `ALL_TERMS` (they need attributes); their own testset brute-forces them and pins R's values and labels (`count_covariates.toml`). Vertex indices are `Integer` everywhere (`_wkey(net::Network{T}, i, j)` converts to the weight dictionary's `Tuple{T,T}`), pinned by the `Network{Int32}` testset. `CountMutualTerm` (every form), `CyclicalTiesTerm`, `NodeOSumTerm`, `NodeISumTerm` declare `requires_directed = true`: `compute` returns 0 on an undirected network, and `CountERGMModel`/`simulate_count_ergm` **refuse** them there with a hint (`NodeSumTerm`, or drop the term) rather than fit an identically-zero column. Coefficient labels come from the two-argument shared generic `name(term, net)`. The weights terms' profiles are sums of clamp ramps (`_ramp_register!`: `clamp(y, a, b) − clamp(old, a, b)` into the same difference array as `_tri_register!`), one two-path search per affected pair rather than one per pair and support value.

3. **Model** -- `CountERGMModel{T,D,TT<:Tuple,R}` mirrors `ERGM.ERGMModel{T,D}`: `network::Network{T,D}`, `terms::TT` (a **Tuple**, so the support profiles fold statically through the `@generated` `_accumulate_conditional!` (the conditional's linear predictor) and `_fill_slab!` (a design slab) — never `map` over the tuple, which boxes from 32 terms on; both pinned at 0 B for a 36-term tuple), `reference::R`; no `directed` field, `is_directed(model)` reads `D`; `show` prints the one-line summary `ERGM.ERGMModel` prints. Constructed from a Tuple, a Vector or a single term. The inner constructor is where the data are **validated**, so `count_mple(model)` and the bootstrap refits inherit every refusal: a two-mode network (`_refuse_two_mode`, ERGM.jl's reasoning — the within-mode dyads would be enumerated as observed zeros; `BipartiteNetwork` methods on the constructor, `fit_ergm_count` and `simulate_count_ergm` give the same message instead of a MethodError), a directed-only term on an undirected network (`_validate_count_terms`), and the counts themselves (`_validate_count_weights`: edges but no `:weight` attribute at all is refused — it would fit as a 0/1 network on which `sum ≡ nonzero` — and so is an edge without `:weight` among weighted ones, naming the first bare edge and how many (a bare edge is a data gap, never a count of 1; R's `response=` never reads a missing value as 1); every value must be an `Integer` or integer-valued `Real`; a negative value only under `DiscUnif2Reference`; the validator returns the smallest count so `_validate_negative_terms` can refuse the terms R refuses on negative data). A binary ERGM.jl term (`ERGM.Edges()`, `ERGM.Mutual()`, …) is refused by `_validate_count_terms` (`_is_count_term`: has a `change_stat_count` method) naming its count analogue (`_count_analogue`), instead of a `MethodError` in the design build; `simulate_count_ergm(terms, net, coefs)` with the arguments swapped is the same named `ArgumentError` as `fit_ergm_count(terms, net)`. `fit_ergm_count(net, terms; weight=:w)` is R's `response="w"`: `_with_weight` fits a `copy` carrying the counts under `:weight`. `has_dyad_dependent(::CountERGMModel)` is a method of the ONE `ERGM.has_dyad_dependent` predicate (never a same-named private); it decides `is_exact`, the `show` caveat, `approximations`, whether default inference is withheld and whether the joint-support probe runs.

4. **Estimation** -- `fit_ergm_count` (alias `ergm_count`; the never-released `fit_count_ergm` was removed and is listed in the docs' "Renamed and removed names" table) builds the model, resolves `method` with `ERGM.resolve_method(method, has_dyad_dependent(model); exact=:mple, mcmc=:mcmle, context="fit_ergm_count")` (`:auto` default: MPLE for a dyad-independent formula, MCMLE otherwise; any other symbol is ERGM's `ArgumentError`), refuses a keyword the chosen estimator does not take (`_check_count_keywords`, reading `Base.kwarg_decl` of `count_mple`/`count_mcmle` — `count_mcmle`'s `se` is excluded since it exists only to refuse; the message names the method that takes it, "pass method=:mple explicitly"), and calls `count_mple` or `count_mcmle`. `show` prints `Method: <method> (<gloss>)` (`_method_gloss`, ERGM's wording). Tests and examples that mean the MPLE of a dyad-dependent formula pass `method=:mple` explicitly (the call sites were found with an instrumented suite run that logged every `:auto` → `:mcmle` resolution). `count_mple` is a proper count MPLE that enumerates each dyad's full conditional P(y_ij=y|rest) ∝ h(y)·exp(θ'Δg(y)) over the support — the reference measure enters the estimator directly — and maximizes it with the shared `NetworkCore.newton_fit` (`ERGM.newton_fit` is the same binding). `loglik` is the maximized pseudo-log-likelihood; the result answers the full StatsAPI surface (`coef stderror vcov confint loglikelihood nobs dof aic bic coeftable`, plus `coefnames` — `_term_names`, R's labels, a fresh vector — pinned with `check_statsapi(...; required=(STATSAPI_VERBS..., :coefnames), strict=true)`; `nobs` is `ERGM.Extension.n_observed_dyads(network)`, ERGM's one dyad count, and `compute`'s zero-dyad counts use `_n_face_dyads`/`_n_empty_dyads` (ERGM's observed dyads plus the masked ones: `compute` reads face values); `aic`/`bic` are pseudo-likelihood criteria; `coeftable` is the `NetworkCore.CoefficientTable` that `show` prints, from the same `z_values`/`p_values`).

   **Compressed design** (`_count_design`): dyads with identical (terms × support) change-statistic slabs share one row with a per-support-value observation count `C[s, r]` and a weight `n_tot[r]` — every dyad of a dyad-independent model is ONE row whatever the network size; the per-dyad build sweep writes into a reused buffer and copies only a new slab (O(unique slabs) allocation, pinned). `_count_derivatives(design, cols, mask)` is the `(ll, grad, hess)` closure over that design with workspaces allocated once (≤ 512 B per evaluation, pinned). `_row_logZ!` is the ONE conditional computation shared by the derivatives, the boundary-mass and the omitted-tail diagnostics.

   **Boundary statistics** (`_count_boundary!`): a statistic at its smallest/largest attainable value on every dyad's conditional support has no finite MPLE. As in R `ergm`, its coefficient is fixed at ∓Inf (SE 0, p 0, excluded from `dof`), each dyad's support is restricted to the values that statistic allows (`mask`), the detection is iterated to a fixed point, and `_warn_count_boundary` says so. A column whose change statistics are all ZERO (a `transitiveweights` on a network with no weighted two-path, a threshold above the support) is constant on every support and so reported at its minimum — R's `ergm.checkextreme.model` on the term's `minval` (0), so the count terms need no separate attainable-range table (ERGM's `_extreme_statistics` reads ERGM term types only). `count_mcmle` applies the same drop (R's `drop=TRUE`): `_fixed_sides(start)` lists the MPLE's ∓Inf columns, `_warn_count_drop` warns R's sentence, the iteration runs on the free columns only (`ERGM.Extension.mcmle_solve` sees `θ[free]`, `labels[free]`, `target[free]`), the sampler runs at ±1e300 on the fixed ones (a value that keeps the statistic at its bound has change 0 and contributes 0; one that moves it gets weight 0), the fixed columns get SE 0, a zero row/column of `vcov`, constant columns in `fit.mcmc.samples`, and `loglik = NaN` (the bridge's dyad-independent reference cannot hold the statistic at its bound; `_fixed_note` says so for an MCMLE fit). Every statistic fixed → `ArgumentError` ("nothing to estimate"). `drop=false` on `count_mple`/`count_mcmle`/`fit_ergm_count` is the strict mode (`_refuse_count_no_drop`, R's `control.ergm(drop=FALSE)` not implemented). Pinned by the 3-cycle drop in "MCMLE: exact against enumeration" (the restricted MLE enumerated over the 729 states) and `count_geometric_drop.toml` (d). `se=:bootstrap`, `simulate_count_ergm` and `gof` refuse a fit with a fixed coefficient.

   **Continuation in the support** (`_support_ladder`/`_ladder_fit`): Newton from θ = 0 on a wide support overshoots (the counting measure's initial conditional is uniform on `0:max_val`), so every fit climbs `0:base`, `0:2·base`, ..., the requested top, each rung warm-started from the one below (`θ0=`); bounded references with a wide support climb it too, and bootstrap refits start at θ̂. When Newton cannot move from a start (singular Hessian at a point-mass conditional, e.g. `BinomialReference(100)`), `_coordinate_ascent_start` (bisection on each coordinate's monotone score) supplies a rescue start; `newton_fit` runs under a `NullLogger` (`_quiet_newton`); `count_mple` itself reports a singular or ill-conditioned final Hessian once (see **Conditioning** below).

   **Error-controlled support**: with `max_val=nothing`, `_adaptive_support_fit` fits at `max(10, 2·max count)`, refits at twice the bound, and stops when **all** of `max|Δθ| ≤ support_tol·SE` (default 1e-3; absolute scale where the SE is NaN/0), the expected number of dyads past the previous bound (`_omitted_tail`) ≤ `support_tol`, and the boundary mass at the new top ≤ `BOUNDARY_MASS_TOL` hold; the larger-bound fit is reported. `support_control ∈ (:bounded, :fixed, :converged, :unconverged, :boundary_mode, :improper)`, `support_stable::Bool` (false for `:unconverged`, `:boundary_mode`, `:improper`) plus `support_delta`/`omitted_tail` (0.0 for a bounded reference, NaN for a fixed `max_val`) record what happened; `:unconverged` (cap `max_doublings=8`) is warned — naming the achieved δ, the final `max_val` and the improper-family explanation — listed in `approximations` and printed. The doubling also **stops at the first rung that is not usable** (`_support_rung_usable`: `converged` and `hessian_cond ≤ _HESSIAN_COND_TOL`), returning `:unconverged` with `support_delta = omitted_tail = NaN`: a collinear design (`sum + greaterthan(2) + atleast(3)`) used to run all 8 doublings, growing the design 2^8×, and then be misdiagnosed as "not normalisable"; now the non-convergence/separation/conditioning warnings carry the diagnosis alone (`count_mple` prints a one-line "stopped at max_val = … because the fit there did not converge" instead), `show`'s support line says "adaptive doubling stopped here", and the "not normalisable" sentence is reserved for a doubling that genuinely exhausted `max_doublings`. Bounded references never loop. The old fixed default was 1.4e-6/3.9e-6 from the exact MLE on `zach`, above the fixture's tolerance; the default path is now pinned against the fixture directly. **The fit is not bit-identical to 0.1.x** (the compressed design changes the summation order); the fixture tolerance (1e-6, reproduced to ~1e-12) is the standard.

   **Convergence** is loud: a final fit that exhausts `maxiter` or cannot move is warned about (naming `maxiter`, `tol` and the pseudo-score norm, with the two ways out), recorded as `converged=false` with `iterations`/`gradient_norm`, listed in `approximations`, printed by `show`, and never `is_exact` — nor is a fit with a `±Inf` coefficient. The coordinate-ascent rescue is tried only when Newton stopped *before* its budget; running out of `maxiter` is the caller's budget and is reported as such. `warn=false` silences every fit diagnostic (boundary, truncation, non-convergence, separation, conditioning) without changing the record; the bootstrap refits use it and report exclusions once, in aggregate.

   **Separation** (`_count_separation_verdict`, NetworkCore's shared verdict): a design on which the pseudo-likelihood is flat along a *combination* of the statistics — `sum + nonzero` when every count is 0 or 1 (`sum − nonzero` at its minimum on every dyad), `sum + atleast(2)` when every count is 0 or 2 — has no finite MPLE, and `_count_boundary!` (one column at a time) cannot see it; Newton "converges" in one step to θ ≈ (−21, 21) with SE 2e4. The count pseudo-likelihood is a conditional logit: `_count_separation_verdict(D, cols, mask)` flattens the compressed design to one row per allowed `(r, s)` (`X[cols, s, r]`, `chosen = C[s, r] > 0`, `strata = r`) and calls `NetworkCore.clogit_separation` — an exact LP verdict certified in rational arithmetic (R's `mple.existence`), on the design actually fitted (after `_count_boundary!` fixed the single boundary columns and restricted `mask`), independent of where Newton stopped (a fit cut off at `maxiter=2` is still declared separated). `_count_mple_fit` computes it on every rung (cheap: q dual rows). A separated fit follows the shared policy: `warn_separation("count_mple", v, names; estimate="MPLE", note=_SEPARATION_NOTE)` (names the terms, R's "The MPLE does not exist!"), `converged=false`, `separated=true`, `separated_terms` (the verdict's inclusion-minimal set), z/p `NaN` via `z_pvalues(θ, se, v)` (in `_count_zp`), `confint` all `NaN`, `separation_caveat(separated_terms)` in `show`/`approximations`, never `is_exact`, `se=:bootstrap` and `count_mcmle` refused, and a separated bootstrap replicate excluded. The adaptive support loop needs no special case: a separated rung is unconverged, so the doubling stops there.

   **Conditioning** (`_hessian_cond`, `_collinear_names`): `hessian_cond` is the 2-norm condition number of the negative pseudo-Hessian over the free coefficients (1 when none is free, `Inf` when singular or non-finite). Above `_HESSIAN_COND_TOL = 1e8` the fit warns (unless separated, whose caveat subsumes it), naming the statistics whose loadings on the smallest-eigenvalue eigenvector exceed 30% of the largest (`collinear`, e.g. `greaterthan.2` and `atleast.3` — the same statistic on integer counts — or `sum` and `nonzero` on a 0/1 network); `show` prints the caveat under `Converged:` and `approximations` lists it. An exactly singular Hessian (`newton_fit` breaks with `converged=false` and NaN SEs) is reported in the same sentence.

   **Improper models: the analytic rule** (`_improper_direction`, `_growth`, `_reference_growth`): everything above examines the dyad conditionals *at the observed network*, so a model whose joint distribution is not normalisable (Poisson + `mutual.product` or a squared-strength term with a positive coefficient: every conditional a proper Poisson, the joint improper) used to be certified `:converged` and then simulated/bootstrapped/GOF-tested on a metastable chain — e.g. 25-actor counts where the MPLE gives θ_product = 0.061 (seed 2) or 0.104 (seed 10), escaping at counts 80–160 while the fit stopped at 20. Under a truncating reference (`_reference_growth`: Poisson `(1, log, −1)` per dyad, i.e. −Y log Y; geometric 0; any other truncating reference → rule silent) the rule evaluates the leading order of `log h + θ'g` along fixed configurations scaled by Y → ∞ (`_configurations`: `:dyad`, `:mutual`, `:outstar`, `:instar` directed; `:dyad`, `:star` undirected; `:all`), summing `θ_k · _growth(term, config, n, directed).c` at the top order: `_Growth(p, lg, c)` = c·Y^p (lg=false) or c·Y log Y (lg=true); `_LINEAR` marks a term known to grow at most linearly (thresholds, minima, triadic weights/ties, `mutual.min/nabsdiff/geom.mean/threshold`, `sum(pow≤1)`); `nothing` (the `AbstractERGMTerm` fallback, i.e. a user term) makes the rule silent. Super-linear: `sum(pow>1)` (m·Y^p), `CMP` (m·Y log Y, so improper beyond θ=1 under Poisson and θ>0 under geometric), `mutual.product` (1 on `:mutual`, n(n−1)/2 on `:all`), the squared strengths (`_strength_squares`, closed forms pinned against `compute` on the configurations themselves). A positive top-order sum along any configuration is a proof of impropriety whatever the data; a negative super-linear term can offset (`nodeOSum` 0.1 with `nodeSum` −0.1 is not flagged). When nothing super-linear contributes and the reference does not decay (the geometric counting measure), the exact LINEAR coefficients decide (`_linear_growth`, `_linear_total`): sums and minima are homogeneous of degree 1 (`sum` m, `mutual.min`/`geom.mean` 1 on `:mutual`, `nabsdiff` −1 on `:dyad`, the two-path terms only on `:all`), thresholds 0, the covariate terms' `form=:sum` unknown (the configuration stays undecided); so `sum + mutual(:min)` with 2θ_sum + θ_mutual > 0 is improper on `:mutual` (closed forms pinned against `compute` at Y and 2Y on every configuration). `count_mple` sets `improper` (skipped for a separated fit), `support_control = :improper` on a converged adaptive path, warns (`_improper_message`), `show` prints `WARNING:` (`_improper_caveat`, recomputed from the stored θ), `approximations` lists it, and `se=:bootstrap`, `simulate_count_ergm(fit)`/`gof` (`_result_support`), `count_mcmle` (start) refuse unless the support is caller-fixed / `max_val=k` is passed. The explicit `simulate_count_ergm(net, terms, θ)` with `max_val=nothing` refuses improper coefficients too. `count_mcmle` evaluates the rule at its final θ as well (`improper` field). Pinned by "Improper models are flagged analytically, whatever the data" (the two seeds, the rule's unit cases, every `ALL_TERMS` entry has a known growth).

   **Joint-support probe** (`_boundary_mode_probe`): after a converged, finite fit of a dyad-dependent model under a truncating reference, the probe starts with EVERY dyad at the top and iterates conditional modes (`_icm_from_top`, deterministic, no draws): a sweep that leaves no dyad at the top → not sticky; a fixed point (or 50 sweeps) with dyads still there → `boundary_mode = true` **only if** that configuration's joint log-weight `log h + θ'g` (`_joint_logweight`) exceeds the observed network's. A proper geometric model with strong reciprocity (θ_sum + θ_mutual > 0 > 2θ_sum + θ_mutual) has the all-top network as a coordinate-wise mode whose weight falls like (2θ_sum + θ_mutual)·Y per pair; it used to be refused and is pinned against ergm.count by `count_geometric_drop.toml` (c) and three reciprocity-heavy 20-actor data sets. On the seed-2/seed-10 `mutual.product` data the modes stick at 0:80–0:160 but weigh less than the data there (the probe is false); at 0:5120 they outweigh it. On the error-controlled path the probe runs on `0:(2^max_doublings · max_val)` — the bound where the adaptive sampler gives up — not on the fitted bound (the seed-2 model: false at 0:20 and 0:80, true at 0:160); a caller-fixed `max_val` is probed as given, an `:unconverged` doubling at its own bound. `_log_reference_vector` builds log h over the wide support in one pass (Poisson's cumulative log-factorial; `log_reference` per value is O(y)), so the probe costs ≈ 0.07 s on zach's `transitiveweights` model at 0:7168. On the adaptive path `support_control` becomes `:boundary_mode` (unless `:improper` already) and `support_stable = false` (a caller-fixed `max_val` stays `:fixed` with `boundary_mode = true`); the fit warns, `show` prints a `WARNING:` line, `approximations` lists it, and `se=:bootstrap`, `simulate_count_ergm(fit)`, `gof` and `count_mcmle` refuse (`simulate_count_ergm(fit; max_val=k)` is the written opt-in to the truncated family). A model improper only beyond the probe's bound, by growth the rule cannot read, is not detected (disclosed).

   **Standard errors and the default presentation** (`se_type`, reported by `NetworkCore.se_method`): ERGM.jl's MPLE pattern. `se=nothing` (default) is `:hessian` with the inference of a dyad-dependent model **withheld**: `z_values`/`p_values` are `NaN` for the finite coefficients (a `∓Inf` one keeps R's z = ∓Inf, p = 0), `inference_withheld = true`, `show` prints the note, `confint` throws an `ArgumentError`, `approximations` records it (pseudo-Hessian Wald coverage simulated at the MLE of the zach model, 300 networks: 0.91/0.98/0.70, bootstrap 0.96/0.97/0.97 on 100 (a scratch simulation outside the repository; when the quoted figures are touched, re-measure, do not copy; a 60-replicate testset pins mean naive SE < 0.75 × empirical sd for `transitiveweights`). An explicit `se=:hessian` is the written opt-in to the naive table (with the "anticonservative" warning). `se=:bootstrap` is a parametric bootstrap on the ONE shared `NetworkCore.bootstrap_cov` loop (same API as `ERGM.mple`: `n_boot`/`boot_burnin`/`boot_interval`/`rng`); replicates without a finite, converged MPLE are returned as NaN, **excluded** from the covariance with a warning, and kept in `boot_replicates`; the warning, `show` and `approximations` (`_boot_exclusion_note`) carry the family's sentence `_BOOT_EXCLUSION_BIAS` verbatim ("The standard errors are conditional on a finite refit: …biased downward."; a local constant with ERGM's text, since ERGM's is internal); the simulating chain is adaptive for an error-controlled fit (the refits enumerate the support the chain ended on) and fixed for a caller-fixed `max_val` (refused if the bound carries more than `BOUNDARY_MASS_TOL` of a conditional). The `se=` keyword is validated by the shared `NetworkCore.check_se`. The point estimates are identical under all three, and they are pseudo-likelihood estimates: `show` says so **under the bootstrap too** (it printed no caveat there before) and `approximations` keeps the point-estimate sentence, naming the zach gap (1.7–1.8 SE from ergm.count's MCMLE) and `method=:mcmle`.

   **MCMLE** (`count_mcmle`): ergm.count's estimator on the exact Gibbs sampler. Start: the MPLE (a `∓Inf` coefficient is R's drop, see **Boundary statistics**; refused with an `ArgumentError` when it is separated (naming the terms), did not converge, or is `improper` or has `boundary_mode` on a non-fixed support). A dyad-independent model returns the exact MPLE relabelled `method = :mcmle` (`mcmc === nothing`). Otherwise **the iteration is ERGM.jl's public `_mcmle_solve`** — never a local loop: `count_mcmle` supplies only the `draw(θ, n)` closure (burn `burnin` sweeps at θ, then `n` retained states of the ONE Gibbs chain — `_simulate_chain(...; stat!, state)`, statistics by `compute`, no network copies — thinned by ERGM's public `_ess_sample` until the Geyer ESS reaches `effective_size`, default `n_samples ÷ 2`; the next draw starts at half the interval reached) and the keywords (`termination=:confidence` — R ergm 4's rule with its sample boost — or `:hotelling`; `gamma0`, `max_step_norm`, `max_n_samples`, `maxiter=60`). One thing is added after the driver returns converged: ONE more draw at θ̂, from which the covariance (`ERGM.Extension.mcmle_covariance`), the MC error and `fit.mcmc.convergence` are computed — the driver's own covariance is that of the sample the last step was taken FROM, which from an MPLE start can sit a standard error away after a two-iteration convergence (measured on zach: SEs up to 9 % below R's, 0.93 of the fixture band; with the draw at θ̂ ≤ 0.38). `se_type = :mcmc`, `se_method = :fisher`; `fit.mcmc` also records `termination`, `termination_p`, `conv_precision`, `conv_confidence` and the adapted `interval`. Which sample is which: `termination_p` is the rule's own test (converged: the test that passed, on the pre-step sample; unconverged: the `:confidence` test re-run by the driver on a fresh sample at the last iterate, or the last iteration's `:hotelling` test); `samples`, `convergence` (t-ratios, Hotelling p, n_eff) and the SEs are always on a sample at the returned θ (the extra draw after convergence, or the driver's fresh draw when unconverged). Non-convergence is worded by `_count_termination_verdict` (ERGM's `_termination_verdict` text): under `:confidence` only "99% equivalence test p … (needs < 0.01; tolerance precision …, n draws), step length γ …", under `:hotelling` "Hotelling p … (n draws), max t-ratio …, step length γ …" — in the warning, `_nonconvergence_caveat` (`show`/`approximations`); pinned for both rules in "MCMLE: exact against enumeration". `loglik` is the log-likelihood itself by path sampling (`_count_bridge_loglik`): θ₀ = θ̂ with the dyad-dependent coordinates zeroed is a dyad-independent model whose log-likelihood at y_obs is the count pseudo-log-likelihood exactly (computed on the compressed design); `log Z` is integrated from θ₀ to θ̂ by ERGM's public `_bridge_integrate` (composite Simpson over `bridge_rungs` segments, rungs on tasks), each grid point its own chain on its own `Xoshiro(seed)` drawn from `rng` up front (thread-count independent); `loglik_mc_se` combines the per-rung batch-means variances with the Simpson weights. It includes the reference measure; R's `logLik` is the same number minus `Σ log h(y_obs) − N·log Σ h` (the testset converts). `objective(fit) = :likelihood`; `is_exact` false. Measured on zach (`sum + nonzero + transitiveweights`), six seeds: mean (0.5973, −4.7781, 0.5600) against R's nine-seed mean (0.5974, −4.7794, 0.5622), seed sd (0.0058, 0.0093, 0.0039) against R's (0.0050, 0.0108, 0.0040), every coefficient within 0.53 and every SE within 0.38 of the fixture band, MC error 3.5–4.4 % of an SE, 2–3 iterations, 20–35 s per fit on 4 threads under load (R: 100 s).

5. **Simulation** -- `simulate_count_ergm` is a **Gibbs sweep** (`_gibbs_sweep!`): every dyad is redrawn once per sweep from its full conditional by the per-dyad kernel `_gibbs_update_dyad!(rng, current, weights, terms, θ, i, j, support, log_h, η, buf)`: `_dyad_conditional!` writes `η[s] = exp(η_s − max η)` with `η_s = log h(y_s) + θ'Δg(old → y_s)` accumulated from the support profiles (`_accumulate_conditional!`, static over the tuple) and returns their sum; `_draw_index(rng, η, total)` is an inverse-CDF draw on those **un-normalised** weights (one `rand`, one pass, no normalising pass); `_set_dyad!` touches the network only when the value actually changes, and an edge exists for every **non-zero** count (`y != 0`, not `y > 0`): under `DiscUnif2Reference(a < 0, b)` a negative draw is stored as a negative `:weight` and read back by `dyad_value` — an early version collapsed such draws to 0, so the sampler drew from a different law than the (correct) estimator fit. **Support of an unbounded reference in the sampler** (`_simulate_chain`, the one chain behind `simulate_count_ergm`, `gof`, the bootstrap, the MCMLE and its path sampling): `max_val=nothing` is ADAPTIVE — the chain carries a `_ChainSupport` (support, `log_h`, the two buffers, counters), starts at `max(start, 10, 2·largest seed count)` and `_gibbs_update_tracked!` doubles the support whenever the conditional puts more than `_GROW_MASS_TOL = 1e-10` on the top value, before drawing; at the cap (`2^max_doublings` × start) it throws `_ChainEscape`, surfaced as an `ArgumentError` naming non-normalisability. `max_val=k` is the truncated family: no growth, and if the largest top-value mass of the retained sweeps exceeds `BOUNDARY_MASS_TOL` the chain warns with the share of draws on the bound (`on_boundary=:warn`, `simulate_count_ergm`) or throws (`on_boundary=:error`: `gof`, the bootstrap, the MCMLE). `_result_support` maps a fit to these (explicit `max_val` → fixed; a `:fixed` fit → its bound; `improper` or `boundary_mode` → refuse; otherwise adaptive from the fit's bound). A bounded reference runs the untracked `_gibbs_sweep!`. The tracked update consumes `rng` exactly as `_gibbs_update_dyad!` (one uniform) — pinned equal draw for draw on a standing support, and 0 B. The explicit form no longer has a literal `max_val=20` (a Poisson mean of 25 simulated 18.0 with 28 % of draws at 20). `gof`'s count-value panel runs over `lo:hi`, every value the observed or a simulated network takes (`_dyad_value_extrema`, `_dyad_value_counts(net, lo, hi)`). Every diagnostic number in `show`, `approximations` and the warnings is formatted by `_fmt3` (`@sprintf "%.3g"`; `round(x, sigdigits=3)` printed `6.969999999999999e-32`), so the docs' quoted outputs are what a user gets. It is *not* a Metropolis toggle chain, so `ERGM.mh_toggle!` deliberately does not apply. `burnin`/`interval` are **sweeps** and default through `_gibbs_defaults(n_dyads)`, the shared `ERGM.Extension.mcmc_defaults` rule converted from toggles to sweeps (`cld(20·n_dyads, n_dyads)` = 20 burn-in sweeps, `cld(max(100, n_dyads ÷ 10), n_dyads)` = 1 between draws beyond ten nodes); `gof`, the bootstrap's `boot_burnin`/`boot_interval` and the MCMLE resolve through the same function. The chain state is `copy(net0)` and every retained draw `copy(current)` — `Base.copy(::Network)` is the ONE copier (graph and attribute dicts duplicated, vertex attributes preserved, no `deepcopy`). The loop snapshots `:weight` once into the typed `Dict{Tuple{T,T},Int}` (`get_edge_attribute(net, :weight, Int)`) and maintains it incrementally, **zeroing rather than deleting** an entry when an edge is removed (`dyad_value` reads it behind `has_edge`, and never deleting keeps the Dict from rehashing on remove/re-add cycles). Masked networks and non-finite coefficients are refused (`require_observed(...; face_ok=false)`); `gof` (a method of `NetworkCore.gof`) inherits both.

## Missing-data contract

No routine exposes a `missing=` keyword: the count MPLE would enumerate an unobserved dyad as an observed row and the Gibbs sweep would condition on a face value nobody observed. `fit_ergm_count`, `count_mple` and `simulate_count_ergm` call `require_observed(net; face_ok=false)` and declare `NetworkCore.missing_policies(f) = (:error,)`; the refusal names `clear_missing_dyads!` and never `:face`. `missing_method(fit) == :rejected`. Two-mode networks are refused on the same principle (`_refuse_two_mode`, at the model constructor and in `_simulate_count`): a within-mode dyad is not a dyad, and enumerating it as an observed zero would be a number computed from data nobody observed.

## Hot paths and their pins

Three loops carry every cost in the package, and each is pinned by `@allocated` in `test/runtests.jl` and again, standalone, by `benchmark/regression_tests.jl`:

- **The Gibbs dyad update** (`_gibbs_update_dyad!`): the conditional and the draw are 0 B for every term in `ALL_TERMS`, directed and undirected, and so is every update that does not *insert an edge* — unchanged, changed on an existing edge, removed — measured as the worst over every dyad of a warmed chain (two sweeps at a dense specification give both weight dictionaries every key they will need, then sweeps at the target coefficients). An insertion is the one mutation that can allocate: Base's `_growat!` reallocates Graphs' sorted adjacency vector on a first-half `insert!` once its front slack is used up — a few hundred bytes amortised over many insertions, independent of the model, measured 0–35 B per insertion and pinned at ≤ 128 B per insertion (+1 KiB) over ten sweeps at 12 and 40 nodes (test) and five at 34 and 100 (gates). A sweep in which no value changes is 0 B without any warm-up, and the benchmark suite's `sweep/n34`, `sweep/n100` medians are 0 allocations. Time: ≈ 0.3–0.6 µs per dyad for `sum + nonzero + mutual.min + nodeOSum` (the committed 0.2.0-dev baseline was 3,108 B and 3.7 µs).
- **The MPLE design build** (`_count_design_rows!`/`_fill_slab!`): 0 B per dyad; the sweep allocates only for new slabs, so a dyad-independent model costs the same bytes at 12 and at 40 nodes (one row) and a dyad-dependent one is proportional to its distinct slabs, never to the dyads.
- **The derivative closure** (`_count_derivatives`): ≤ 512 B per `(ll, grad, hess)` evaluation whatever the rows and support (192 B measured).
- **Scaling**: with the mean degree held fixed, a sweep at n = 100 must cost at most 2× more *per dyad* than at n = 34 (`benchmark/benchmarks.jl` `SCALING` line and the gates' last testset; 0.8× measured) — the profiles are O(degree), so the sweep is O(dyads).
- **Static folds**: `_accumulate_conditional!` and `_fill_slab!` are 0 B for a 36-term tuple (above Base's 32-element `map` limit).

`benchmark/benchmarks.jl` also times `fit/zach_default` (the fixture model on the error-controlled path, ≈ 0.29 ms, 26 KiB), `fit/zach_maxval60` (≈ 1.0 ms, 56 KiB), `derivatives/zach` and `sweep/n34`, `sweep/n100`, and prints the `BENCHJL` lines `tools/run_benchmarks.jl` in the site repo consumes.

A `PrecompileTools` workload at the end of the module (first the README Quick Start call for call — its default `method=:auto` is the MCMLE, which the earlier MPLE-only workload left to compile at the user's first call: 4.4 s of compilation, now 0.1 s; then two 6-node networks, both directednesses, the Poisson default path, a bounded reference with a dyad-dependent term, `show`/`coeftable`/`confint`, `simulate_count_ergm`, `gof`, a guarded two-replicate bootstrap and a guarded tiny MCMLE under a devnull logger) takes the first `fit_ergm_count` in a fresh session from ≈ 2.2 s to ≈ 0.01 s and the first `gof` from ≈ 0.5 s to ≈ 0.01 s (package precompile 2 s → 8 s).

## Golden fixtures (statnet ergm / ergm.count)

Five fixtures, all generated by checked-in R scripts (the two newer ones: `count_geometric_drop.toml` from `test/fixtures/r/count_geometric_drop.R` — (c) a proper geometric `sum + mutual(min)` model in the reciprocity regime, (d) `transitiveweights` dropped at -Inf on a perfect matching, nine ergm.count seeds each, the `count_mcmle.toml` band; `count_covariates.toml` from `test/fixtures/r/count_covariates.R` — the covariate terms' values and labels on a directed and an undirected network at 1e-9, and an exact-MLE fit against ergm.count's five-seed MCMLE) and loaded with NetworkCore.jl's `load_golden` (which throws without `[provenance]`):

- **`test/fixtures/count_mcmle.toml`** (`test/fixtures/r/count_mcmle.R`, ~10 min on 3 cores): **the MCMLE**. Two dyad-dependent Poisson-reference models — `zach ~ sum + nonzero + transitiveweights(min,max,min)` and a seeded 14-actor directed count matrix with built-in reciprocity `~ sum + nonzero + mutual(min)` (frozen as an edge list) — each fit by ergm.count at `control.ergm` defaults under nine seeds (per-seed coefficients/SEs, mean, sd, bridge log-likelihoods) plus one long-chain fit (`hp_*`, samplesize 16384); the script `stopifnot`s that the nine-seed mean agrees with the long fit. Both sides are Monte-Carlo MLEs, so a Julia fit must lie within `band_sd · sd_R · √(1 + 1/9)` of R's mean, floored at 0.1·SE (coefficients and SEs), and its log-likelihood — converted to R's reference-relative convention — within `band_sd·√2·sd_R` floored at 1.0. The testset fits zach once (seed 1, the default path) and the directed model under three seeds, also asserts `mcse/se ≤ 0.05` and that the MPLE of the zach model is 1.5–2.0 R-SEs from R's mean on `sum` and `transitiveweights` (the 1.7/1.8 the docs quote). Do not widen the band to make a seed pass; a miss is an estimator bug or an R change.
- **`test/fixtures/count_terms.toml`** (`test/fixtures/r/count_terms.R`, ergm 4.12.0 / ergm.count 4.1.3): **term parity**. The summary statistics *and the coefficient names* R prints for `sum`, `nonzero`, `greaterthan(2)`, `greaterthan(4)`, `atleast(3)`, `transitiveweights(min,max,min)`, `cyclicalweights(min,max,min)`, `smallerthan(2)`, `equalto(3)`, `ininterval(1,3)` (all four bracket forms), `mutual(form=min/nabsdiff/geometric/product)`, and `greaterthan(-1)`/`atleast(0)` (thresholds at or below 0 count the zero dyads: 561 on zach), on `zach` (undirected, frozen as a valued edge list) and on a seeded 8-actor directed count matrix (frozen as an edge list), at 1e-9 (the 0.2 additions `sum(pow=2)`, `sum(pow=1/2)`, `atmost(2)`, `atmost(0)`, `CMP` are separate `*_added_summary` keys computed after every random draw of the script, so the older rows are byte-identical; on the negative network `sum(pow=2/3)`, `atmost(0/-1)` are pinned and R's `Inf` for `CMP` / `NaN` for `sum(pow=1/2)` are frozen as the behaviour ERGMCount refuses) — deterministic functions of the graph, so any disagreement is a term-formula bug; plus a 3-actor network with a **0-valued tie** (`set.edge.value` on an existing tie, frozen with the zero in its edge list), on which R's `nonzero` is 1 and the threshold terms count the tie with the empty dyads — `compute` reads `dyad_value`, never `ne(net)`, so it agrees. The testset rebuilds all three networks from the TOML alone, compares `[name(t, net) ...]` to R's names exactly and `compute` row by row. `mutual(form="threshold")` is deliberately **not** pinned: ergm 4.12.0's own `summary()` fails at C model initialisation, the script freezes that error (`r_error_mutual_threshold`) and stops if it ever starts working (so the row gets added), and `CountMutualTerm(:threshold)` is tested by hand value. There is no estimation in this fixture (`count_mcmle.toml` is the estimation fixture for dyad-dependent models).
- **`test/fixtures/zach_poisson.toml`** (`test/fixtures/r/zach_poisson.R`): the **exact-MLE** fixture. Model: `zach ~ sum + nonzero`, `reference = ~Poisson`, on ergm.count's bundled Zachary karate-club network (counts 0-7).

`sum + nonzero` is **dyad-independent on purpose**: each dyad is then an independent draw from a two-parameter law with an **exact MLE**, and `count_mple`'s dyad-conditional enumeration *is* the likelihood — so ERGMCount.jl computes that exact MLE and is held to **1e-6**, not to "within Monte-Carlo error". It reproduces it to ~2e-12, both at the pinned `max_val=30` and on the **default path** (adaptive doubling 14 → 28); the testset also shows that the old fixed default (14) misses the tolerance, so the doubling is what earns it.

Two things the fixture had to get right, and both are worth knowing:

- **The golden value is solved analytically**, not with `optim`. The score equations collapse to one monotone scalar root-find. `optim(BFGS, reltol=1e-14)` — the obvious way — landed **7e-6** from the true optimum, *above* the tolerance it was meant to police. A reference number only good to 7e-6 cannot hold anyone to 1e-6. The frozen residual score is ~1e-14.
- **Truncation is measured, not assumed.** The Poisson reference is unbounded; the estimator enumerates `0:max_val` and the exact MLE truncates nothing, so they estimate the same thing only if the boundary mass is negligible. The fixture freezes `P(y > 30) = 6.9e-23` under the fitted law against a smallest-used conditional probability of 2.3e-3 — nineteen orders of magnitude — and the testset re-asserts ERGMCount.jl's own reported `boundary_mass` against that bound, so a future `max_val` that started to bite goes **red** rather than quietly widening the gap.

`ergm.count`'s own fit is MCMLE (statnet has no valued MPLE) and sits **0.0103** from the exact MLE — *further* than ERGMCount.jl does. It is frozen as a cross-check on R, not as the reference standard.

## Conventions

- All term structs subtype `AbstractERGMTerm` and must implement `name()`, `compute()` and `change_stat_count()`; declare `is_dyad_dependent(::T) = false` only when the change statistic depends on nothing but `old`/`new`, and `requires_directed(::T) = true` when the statistic is identically zero on an undirected network.
- Edge weights are stored as the `:weight` edge attribute on `Network` objects; absent weight means binary (0/1).
- `fit_ergm_count` is the standardized entry point (ecosystem `fit_<model>` naming); `ergm_count` is the R-faithful alias — the same function (`const`). Like `ERGM.fit_ergm`, its default is `method=:auto` (R's rule, `ERGM.resolve_method`); R requires `reference=` where this defaults to Poisson (disclosed). No deprecation shims: a removed development name goes in the docs' "Renamed and removed names" table (`docs/src/api/estimation.md`). Keyword vocabulary: `maxiter`, `n_sim`, `rng`, `n_boot`, `n_samples`, `burnin`, `interval`, `bridge_rungs`, `bridge_samples`.
- README "Not implemented", `docs/src/index.md` "Not implemented" and CHANGELOG "Known limitations" carry the same items; the CHANGELOG is release notes (no internal process references) — add a short entry per behaviour change, not a narrative. 0.1.0 is described as "development version, never released".
- The test suite carries the ecosystem's workflow testset ("Workflows reconstruct the ecosystem layout from [sources]": it runs the workflow's own layout step in check-only mode, with `JULIA_LOAD_PATH`/`JULIA_PROJECT` cleared because `Pkg.test`'s sandbox hides the stdlibs; `layout_siblings` in the test preamble decides whether the sibling checkouts named by `[sources]` are beside the package — none present (a lone checkout or a registry install) → the step is not run, an `@info` says why and a `@test_skip` records it; any present → it runs and must find exactly the expected set, so a partial layout fails; the predicate is pinned on a `mktempdir`) and an Aqua testset (`test_all` with `ambiguities=false`, then `Test.detect_ambiguities(ERGMCount, ERGM, NetworkCore)` empty); test-only deps have `[compat]` entries.
- All randomness flows through `rng::AbstractRNG` keywords (default `Random.default_rng()`); never call bare `rand()`.
- Julia >= 1.12 is required (see `[compat]` in Project.toml).
- Tests in `test/runtests.jl` include brute-force verification of every `change_stat_count` (`ALL_TERMS` holds every term, every `mutual` form and every `ininterval` bracket form, so a new term goes there and is then brute-force-, profile- and 0-B-tested for free), the R-parity term testset (hand values, R labels, the `SmallerthanTerm` boundary case), the term-parity golden fixture, hand-computed term values, Poisson-rate recovery of the MPLE, estimation↔simulation round trips, the boundary-statistic limits in closed form (zero-truncated Poisson, restricted binomial), the support profiles against the per-value change statistics for every term, both directednesses and every support value, `@allocated` pins on the design sweep, the derivative closure, the Gibbs dyad update and a whole sweep (0 B on a grown chain at two sizes), the sweep-count pin of the dyad-scaled defaults (a default-path draw equals the spelled-out 20/1 one and differs from a 19-sweep one; `gof` and the bootstrap inherit it), `NetworkCore.check_statsapi(fit; strict=true)` on three fits, a fresh-process thread-count-independence check of the bootstrap, loud non-convergence (`maxiter=1` on a single-rung support), the golden fixture on both the pinned and the default support path (plus its unstable-support twin), the sampler's support (adaptive means, fixed-bound warning, the improper-model refusals, the boundary-mode probe on a 12-actor `mutual.product` reproduction, tracked ≡ plain draws and 0 B), the analytic impropriety rule (the two 25-actor seeds, the probe blind at 0:20 and sighted at the cap, the rule's unit cases), the withheld-inference presentation, the MCMLE against exact enumeration on a 3-actor network (729 states: maximizer, standard errors, log-likelihood) and against the ergm.count fixture.
